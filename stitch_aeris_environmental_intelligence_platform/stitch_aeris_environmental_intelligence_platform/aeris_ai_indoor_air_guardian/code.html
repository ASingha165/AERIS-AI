Timed Micro-Ventilation

Expected Gain: 30–45% CO₂ & VOC Drop

Open north-facing window a 4-inch crack strictly between 1:30 PM – 3:00 PM when thermal convective mixing creates the lowest ambient outdoor PM2.5 of the day. Never ventilate during dawn inversions (<9:00 AM).

Difficulty: Very Easy
Cost: ₹150 (Mop Cloth) verified Evidence: Very High
cleaning_services

Damp Microfiber Mopping

Expected Gain: -70% Settled Dust Resuspension

Dry broom sweeping launches fine settled particles straight into the human breathing zone where they float for >2 hours. Damp microfiber mechanically locks electrostatically charged particles without re-aerosolizing.

Difficulty: Easy Daily Habit
Cost: ₹299 (Seal Strip) science Evidence: High

Door Bottom Weather-Strip

Expected Gain: Blocks ~40% Infiltration

Standard 15mm gaps beneath apartment doors act as vacuum injectors for dusty stairwells and street corridors. Installing an adhesive silicone draft sweep isolates the room envelope effortlessly.

Difficulty: Moderate DIY One-Time (10 min)
Cost: ₹0 (Zero Spend) science Evidence: High

Kitchen Boundary Barrier

Expected Gain: Prevents 300+ µg/m³ Peak

Frying with mustard or sunflower oil generates concentrated ultrafine organic carbon plumes. Always close interior corridor doors and crack the kitchen service window to feed exhaust suction directly.

Difficulty: Easy Immediate Protocol

lightbulb Atmospheric Myth Buster
NASA Clean Air Study Re-analysis
Myth

“Filling your bedroom with indoor snake plants or peace lilies cleans PM2.5 and eliminates hazardous air.”

Reality

The seminal 1989 NASA experiment tested plants sealed within tiny, hermetic chamber volumes specifically measuring trace volatile organic chemical breakdown over days. In real apartments with baseline air-exchange, plants exhibit virtually 0.0% filtration efficiency on physical PM2.5 particulate matter.

arrow_right_alt Scientific Verdict: Keep plants for aesthetics, but rely exclusively on physical barrier sealing and HEPA-grade mechanical filtration.

Source: Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology Peer Reviewed
